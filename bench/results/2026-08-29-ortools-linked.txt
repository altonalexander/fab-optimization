VERDICT: the CP-SAT formulation is sound. It beats greedy at every scale, with
0 hard-constraint violations on both sides, and assigns strictly more lots while
costing strictly less. The direction and rough magnitude of the README claim
survive contact with a real solver.

WHAT DID NOT REPRODUCE, AND WHY

1. The exact +34.4% is not reproduced; this run gives 21-30% depending on scale.
   The instances are not the same. The deleted Python harness reported 859
   feasible pairs at 200x60; the C++ generator in src/test_main.cpp produces
   1625 pairs at 200x50. Denser feasibility gives greedy more good choices and
   narrows the gap. Two different instances, so the two numbers were never
   comparable. This is a generator difference, not a formulation error.

2. The operational budget number in dispatch/README.md is WRONG on this build.

   README:  "At 400 lots a 0.25s budget returns no incumbent. At 1s it is optimal."
   Measured at 400 lots:
       budget 0.25s -> no incumbent, fallback to greedy (13590020)
       budget 1.0s  -> no incumbent, fallback to greedy (13590020)
       budget 2.0s  -> 10700860, +21.3%
       budget 3.0s  -> 10700860
       budget 5.0s  -> 10700860

   The real threshold is between 1s and 2s, not at 1s. A tactical cycle tuned to
   the documented 1s would silently run greedy forever while reporting that
   CP-SAT was enabled -- the worst kind of wrong, because the backend table says
   "cpsat linked" and it would be telling the truth.

   Corrected guidance: the tactical cycle must allow >=2s of solve time at 400
   lots on this hardware, and the figure must be re-measured per deployment
   rather than inherited. The greedy fallback is load-bearing, as claimed.
